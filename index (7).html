02 // WHO IS BEHIND FAZWA

INTRODUCING FAWAZ

My name is Muhammed Fawaz P S, but most people know me as "Fazwa." I'm 16, from Old Lakkidi in Palakkad, Kerala — currently in Plus One at NSSKPT VHS School, and pursuing a JDS (Junior Software Developer) course on the side.

You've probably seen the name FAZWA across my projects. Here's how it started.

HOW I ENTERED ROBOTICS

It didn't start with a big lab or expensive equipment — it started at school. In 10th standard I joined Little Kites, my school's computer club. The classes, activities and camps there gave me my first real sense of what robotics actually was.

From there I kept going on my own — YouTube, Google, AI tools, trial and error. My first real build was a small phone-controlled mini car, put together mostly by researching and making mistakes until it worked.

THE CAR THAT BECAME MANY PROJECTS

After that project, I took the car apart instead of leaving it as one finished thing. I listed every component I had and started asking: what else can I build with these? Most new ideas only needed one or two extra parts, so I'd buy those — and realized that combining what I already had with a few new components meant I could build something bigger. One project slowly became another. Then another.

Search → Learn → Build → Break → Fix → Improve → Repeat.

Every project taught me something — sometimes from a successful experiment, sometimes because it completely failed. Every failure gave me another question to research, every question another idea, and every idea another reason to build something new. That's how robotics, electronics, programming and web development grew for me — one project at a time. I call it The Mass Entry of Fawaz.

I DIDN'T LEARN EVERYTHING ALONE

My brother has always been there through this — helping with ideas, pushing me to try new things, and giving me my first real understanding of web development and digital projects. But the actual learning — researching, experimenting, breaking things, figuring it out — was always mine to do.

06 // BUILD LOG

WELCOME ROBOT

"I wanted to build a robot that doesn't simply stand at an expo — I wanted it to actually welcome people."

IDEA→ DESIGN→ CIRCUIT→ CODE→ BODY→ TEST→ EXPO
#001Welcome Robot
EXPO SUCCESSFUL
LOADING 3D MODEL…
DRAG TO ROTATE · SCROLL TO ZOOM

A foam-bodied expo greeter robot that waves hello with a servo-driven arm, blinks through animated LED-matrix eyes, and plays a welcome jingle — all triggered by two push buttons on its back panel. The head was later redesigned into a compact dual-matrix version that scrolls "WELCOME" and my name across its two 8×8 displays before looping into circle-and-square eye animations.

TOTAL HEIGHT
37–40 cm
HEAD (W×H×D)
16×14×10 cm
BODY (W×H×D)
22×20×11 cm
ARM REACH
70° swing
ARM LENGTH
15–18 cm
POWER
5V · 2A
FEATURES
  • Servo-driven right arm waves a 70° hand salam on greeting
  • 3× 8×8 LED matrix eyes with expressive blink animation
  • DFPlayer Mini + speaker for welcome voice line & songs
  • Two rear push buttons — one for greeting, one for music
  • Rev B head: dual-matrix scroll text "WELCOME" + name, looping into circle/square eye animation
  • Flexible ball-joint neck on the redesigned head
CORE PARTS
  • Arduino Uno
  • DFPlayer Mini
  • 3× 8×8 LED Matrix (MAX7219)
  • SG90 / MG90S Servo Motor
  • 3W–5W Speaker
  • 2× Push Buttons
  • MicroSD card (MP3 files)
  • 5V 2A power supply
  • 5mm foam board / PVC sheet body
  • Hot glue, acrylic paint & stickers
