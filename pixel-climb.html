PROJECT 10 // PIXEL CLIMB

PIXEL
CLIMB

Hill Climb Racing OLED Game · NodeMCU handheld

ONGOING

The TEAM FAZWA Hill Climb Racing OLED Game is a compact handheld gaming device built with a NodeMCU ESP8266, an SSD1306 OLED display, two push buttons and a battery pack. Inspired by Hill Climb Racing, it recreates a simple hill-driving game on a tiny monochrome screen.

It is controlled with physical buttons, so no phone or touchscreen is needed, and it gives hands-on practice in electronics, embedded programming and basic game development.

2 BUTTONS→NODEMCU ESP8266→128×64 OLED
  • NodeMCU ESP8266 — main controller. Runs the game, reads buttons, calculates movement and sends graphics to the OLED.
  • SSD1306 OLED, 128 × 64 — shows the car, hills, fuel bar, coins, distance, menus and game-over screen over I²C.
  • Two push buttons — left for brake / reverse, right for accelerate.
  • Battery pack, 2 × 3.7V cells — portable power. In series: 7.4V nominal, up to 8.4V fully charged.
  • Power switch — turns the device on and off, placed between battery and regulator.

On start, the NodeMCU initializes the OLED and shows the menu. The buttons are read by the controller and turned into actions. Right button accelerates, left button brakes or reverses, and the game logic works out speed, gravity, hill slope and jumping.

Distance, coins, fuel and score depend on the features finished in the final sketch. When fuel runs out or the car crashes, the game-over screen can appear.

Do not connect the battery pack directly to VIN. A 7.4V pack can reach 8.4V when fully charged. Use a suitable regulator / buck converter and verify its output before connecting it to the NodeMCU.
  • A 2S lithium-ion pack needs proper 2S protection (BMS) and a matching 8.4V charger.
  • Never use damaged, swollen or overheated cells, and never short the battery.
  • Disconnect battery power while uploading code over USB.
  • Upload an OLED test sketch and confirm the display works.
  • Connect both push buttons and test the inputs.
  • Verify the battery holder configuration and output voltage.
  • Install battery protection and a voltage regulator.
  • Check the game code fits the NodeMCU memory, then upload it.
  • Test controls, rendering, movement, fuel and collision logic.
  • Mount everything on a compact baseboard, insulate solder joints and protect the battery terminals.
